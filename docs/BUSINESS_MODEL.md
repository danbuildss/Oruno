# Business model

Oruno is free while we prove people use it. Revenue comes from the people and organisations who fund drops and from apps that build on us, not from claimers.

## By phase

| Phase | Revenue | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–1 | None | We fund Drop #1 ourselves (about $100–200) |
| 2 | Optional small drop fee | Test whether funders accept a fee |
| 3 | Drop fee + organisation plans | First real revenue |
| 4 | API usage pricing | Per active user or per volume for apps |
| 5–6 | Pool fees, enterprise contracts | Onchain pools, large organisations, agents |

## Revenue streams

| Stream | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| **Drop fee** | A flat fee per drop or a small % of funded amount for running it |
| **Organisation plans** | Branded drops, eligibility lists, approvals, reports, team access |
| **API usage** | Apps that use Oruno to fund their users' AI pay per active user or per volume |
| **Supply margin** | Part of the gap between provider price and list price. Median Claude discount on Surplus is 20–35%. Never the core model, since provider prices move |
| **Enterprise / custom** | Ongoing AI allowances for staff, students or communities; dedicated support |

## Principles

- **Claimers never pay.** That is the product.
- **Fees are visible.** Funders see exactly what goes to AI and what goes to Oruno.
- **Margin must not depend on one provider's discount.** If Surplus prices change, the funder's bill changes, not our model.
- **Open-source core, paid hosting.** The engine is open; the hosted service, organisation features and support are what customers pay for.

## Costs

| Item | Early estimate |
| --- | --- |
| Drop #1 credit | $100–200 (one-off) |
| Acceptance-test key | $20 (one-off) |
| Hosting, database, email, domain | Under $50/month |
| Provider fees | Per-request flat fee to be confirmed with Surplus |

## Signals to watch

- People other than us funding drops.
- Funders running a second drop.
- Organisations asking for branded or private drops.
- Apps asking for an API.
